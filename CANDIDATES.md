# Candidates — One Word for Your Wi-Fi

Gate: 1–20 characters, one word. Judge criteria, most important first:
(1) one word describing Wi-Fi as the entrant lives it; (2) funny at once;
(3) feels true to how home Wi-Fi actually behaves; (4) avoids the words
everyone reaches for, finds a surprising angle; (5) works as a single word
with no context needed, spelled so a reader gets it instantly.

## The 8 candidates (all composed fresh in this session)

1. "Buffering" (9 chars) — Score 16/25. True and instantly understood, but
   the first word everyone reaches for; no surprise.
2. "Ghosting" (8 chars) — Score 22/25. The Wi-Fi ghosts you mid-call, exactly
   like a bad date. Funny at once, painfully true, and a fresh angle on a
   familiar annoyance. Strongest of the eight.
3. "Moody" (5 chars) — Score 15/25. True, but flat; it describes a feeling,
   not a joke.
4. "Selective" (9 chars) — Score 17/25. "It works when it wants to" is true,
   but the word itself is polite rather than funny.
5. "Fickle" (6 chars) — Score 15/25. Accurate, but sounds like a thesaurus
   entry; no laugh.
6. "Vanishing" (9 chars) — Score 16/25. True of a signal that drops, but
   reads as a description, not a punchline.
7. "Teasing" (7 chars) — Score 14/25. The bars appear and disappear, but the
   word is too gentle to land.
8. "Part-time" (9 chars) — Score 19/25. "My Wi-Fi works part-time" is funny
   and true, and it travels well — but it is a close second to Ghosting, which
   names the exact betrayal (mid-call disappearance) rather than the schedule.

## Pick

Candidate 2 — "Ghosting". It is the only candidate where the joke, the truth
and the surprise are the same word: everyone has been ghosted by a signal
that was full bars a second ago.

## Revision

First draft: "ghosting" (lowercase, 8 chars).

Revised final: "Ghosting" (8 chars). The winning word becomes the sponsor's
network name, and a network name reads as a name — capitalised, it stands
alone as a label rather than a stray lowercase word. Verified: main.py exits
